\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied multi-turn reasoning in two settings, both analysed under protocols
frozen before data collection.

\begin{itemize}
  \item On short sharded math tasks, retaining the assistant's own replies
    reduced final success relative to an information-matched context without
    them.
  \item In 100-turn dialogues with calibrated difficulty, accuracy collapsed
    within about twenty turns under accumulated history, while a verified state
    summary preserved it across all three models.
  \item None of the examined signals gave robust, model-general early warning of
    these failures. This covers the measurable reasoning-quality metrics, a
    paired process measure, and single-stream drift detectors.
\end{itemize}

Long interactions can thus degrade LLM reasoning in ways that are invisible to
single-stream quality monitoring. Compact, verified state representations and
comparative, aggregated monitoring are the most promising directions for
building reliable multi-turn systems.
