\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Across two frozen studies, retaining an LLM's own replies lowered success on
short multi-turn tasks, and accumulated history collapsed accuracy within about
twenty turns of 100-turn state-tracking dialogues, even at matched difficulty,
while a verified state summary preserved it. The loss was silent: no examined
quality metric, paired readiness measure or calibrated single-stream detector
gave robust, model-general early warning, and coherence and stability often
held or rose as accuracy fell. LLM-based expert systems should therefore manage
task state explicitly and monitor across sessions or against counterfactual
re-queries. Future work should test token-level and counterfactual uncertainty
signals, separate the length, state-load and error components of the collapse,
and evaluate state representations that models maintain and verify themselves.
