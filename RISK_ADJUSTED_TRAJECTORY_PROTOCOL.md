# RAT-v1: Risk-Adjusted Controlled Trajectory Study

RAT-v1 is a new protocol, not Section 70 and not a relabelling of any result
through Section 69. It starts from the negative early-warning finding and asks
a more defensible prospective question: while a model is still correct on a
problem family, do its current non-CQ measurements predict its first error on
the next, structurally harder member of that family?

## Design

The benchmark contains 60 independent problem families and six nested levels
per family. Twenty families use ordered arithmetic, twenty use weighted
shortest paths and twenty use precedence-constrained scheduling. Every answer
is generated by an exact solver. Increasing a level extends the preceding
problem rather than replacing it with an unrelated question.

The frozen complexity profile is deliberately nonlinear. Arithmetic levels
contain 2/4/7/11/16/22 ordered operations; graph and scheduling levels contain
5/7/10/14/19/25 nodes or tasks. Shortest-path distractors are always more
expensive than the nested backbone, and each scheduling boundary extends the
current critical path while intermediate tasks may branch.

Families, never individual items, are assigned to calibration, development or
holdout. Each template contributes 8/6/6 families to those splits. This gives
24 calibration, 18 development and 18 untouched holdout families, comprising
144/108/108 items. No family may cross a split.

The core measurement arm uses one primary response and three prospectively
stored, meaning-preserving prompt variants. CQ is the outcome; semantic-v1 RS,
LS and ES are the primary predictors. CS and SS are optional because they are
identifiable only when a provider-model cell first demonstrates genuine K
repeat diversity under a separately frozen capability gate.

## Primary Analysis

For each model-family trajectory, the risk set contains levels reached while
all preceding levels remain correct. Metrics at level `t` forecast the first
CQ failure at level `t+1`. The baseline discrete-time hazard model contains
trajectory level, task template and model. The full model adds RS, LS and ES.
All tuning and feature decisions use calibration/development families only.

The primary confirmatory quantity is the family-clustered holdout log-loss
improvement of the full model over the baseline. The early-warning claim is
supported only if the family-bootstrap 95% interval is above zero in at least
two preregistered models after the frozen multiplicity correction. Brier score,
AUPRC, calibration and lead level are secondary outcomes.

Ridge strength is chosen from `C={0.01, 0.1, 1, 10}` with family-grouped
validation. Development uses four outer and three inner grouped folds. The
holdout comparison uses 2,000 family-cluster bootstrap samples, seed 20260926,
one-sided alpha 0.05 and Holm correction across the three model-specific
tests. The analysis and calibration-gate source hashes are part of the
structural preflight, so changing executable inference code breaks the gate.

## Scientific Boundaries

RAT-v1 does not erase the Section 69 result. It tests a new hypothesis under a
design that supplies the temporal and within-family structure missing from the
independent-question streams. A failure remains publishable only as a rigorous
null result; thresholds, included levels and predictors cannot be changed after
holdout outcomes are opened.

The structural preflight and the separately frozen calibration operational
preflight both pass without model calls. The calibration configuration is
limited to the 24 calibration families and preregisters GPT-4o-mini, Llama-4
Scout and Mistral-7B through the accepted ASU endpoints. Each item uses K=1
and L=3, so the ceiling is 576 calls per model, 1,728 calls total and 1,327,104
maximum output tokens at 768 tokens per call. Resume is mandatory.

## Calibration Outcome

The approved calibration run completed on 2026-09-27. All three models
produced 576 records without failed calls or token truncation. The frozen
operational gate nevertheless failed. GPT-4o-mini and Llama-4 Scout met the
answer, event-density and predictor-health criteria but exceeded the strict
eight-numbered-line contract on 47 and 51 calls. Mistral-7B additionally had
only 0.398 strict final-marker observability, insufficient level-one entry and
no censored families. The original gate remains failed and was not relaxed.

A separately frozen, call-free diagnostic then tested whether RS, LS and ES
improved family-grouped next-level failure prediction for GPT-4o-mini and
Llama-4 Scout. The combined nested-CV log-loss improvement was -0.0215, with
only one of four outer folds positive. GPT-4o-mini alone was positive (+0.0341;
3/4 folds), while Llama-4 Scout was negative (-0.0248; 2/4 folds). The frozen
diagnostic therefore failed all continuation checks. No development or holdout
model calls are authorised; the result is calibration evidence, not a
confirmatory claim.

A final, prospectively enumerated calibration-only ablation evaluated all seven
non-empty subsets of RS, LS and ES. No subset met the cross-model continuation
rule. RS was strong for GPT-4o-mini (+0.0738; 4/4 positive folds) but essentially
null for Llama-4 Scout (+0.00002; 2/4). LS showed the opposite pattern: Llama
was positive (+0.0145; 3/4), while GPT was negative (-0.0114; 1/4). Thus the
calibration evidence supports model-specific metric heterogeneity rather than
a transportable early-warning feature set. RAT-v1 stops at calibration and the
untouched development and holdout splits remain closed.
